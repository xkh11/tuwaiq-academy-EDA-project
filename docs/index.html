02 · Problem Definition

One dataset, two people asking different questions

The host
"If I list a property in Bologna, what should I do?"
  1. Who controls the market: single hosts or professionals?
  2. Do professional hosts charge more?
  3. Where is the competition strongest?
  4. Which price range gets the most demand?
  5. Does a higher price mean less demand?
The guest
"If I visit Bologna, where should I stay and what will I pay?"
  1. What does a typical night cost?
  2. Which neighbourhoods are cheapest and most expensive?
  3. How much more is an entire home than a private room?
  4. Can I find short stays?
  5. Where are the listings on the map?

Demand is measured with reviews in the last 12 months, because the data has no booking numbers.

06 · Conclusion

Bologna's Airbnb market rewards the middle

Most listings sit in two central neighbourhoods, a few professional hosts run over a quarter of them, and mid-priced places attract the most guests.

What I learned

  • Cleaning comes first: 518 missing prices and 44 extreme values had to be handled before any average made sense.
  • For skewed prices, the median describes a typical night better than the mean.
  • A model must be compared with a baseline to be honest.

Limitations

  • One snapshot (September 2026), so no seasons.
  • Listed prices, not what guests paid.
  • Reviews only approximate demand.
  • The price model explains only 16% of price differences.

Future work

  • Compare seasons and other Italian cities.
  • Use the detailed file: review scores, amenities, bedrooms.
  • Improve the price model with those features.
